Furry conventions happen nearly every weekend these days, but the truly large events
like [Furry Weekend Atlanta](),  [Midwest FurFest](), and [Anthrocon]() stand alone. With Anthrocon reaching beyond
20,000 in attendance, there is something magic about The World's Largest Furry Convention.

I am often asked by well-meaning normies to explain the Fandom. And though nerd culture has rocketed into the
mainstream, and Queer culture has gained wide acceptance, it's still difficult to give outsiders a whole picture.

## On The Ground

"The City Loves Us"

You'll hear this refrain from locals and long-time attendees. Anthrocon overflows and spills across several city blocks,
turning the downtown core into a Furry street faire. The waterfront becomes a 24/7 gathering place for parties, art, and
socializing. Locals come to visit and mingle with Furries from all over the world.

As a Furry, the experience feels coming home. To hang out with so many like-minded community members and
unapologetically enjoy those "hell ya" high-five moments without needing to pause the party to explain or justify what
we're doing. To stumble in and out of interesting stuff happening, or getting exposed to your next hyper-focus interest
from a stranger doing a cool thing.

## Inside The Center

Anthrocon provides all the features anyone would expect from a convention -- dealers, dances, raves, auctions, art,
panels -- but executed at a scale that few events manage. A legion of volunteers and staff keep the experience moving
mostly-smoothly throughout the event.

Thanks to the AV team for hosting a BTS tour of the main-stage setup with their staff and equipment. For every killer DJ
set, or jaw dropping Floor Wars performance, AV was working hard to make that happen

## But How?

The weather this year was uniquely oppressive: high 90s in both temperature and humidity. Even after dark, it was often
as hot outside the fursuit as in. Which opens the common question refrain from outsiders: "how can you do this?". And
even though any extreme-effort hobby gets similar questions, it's hard to read-in suburban Americans enough to fully
answer.

For most of us we can peel away an onion of reasons why we traveled to Pittsburgh for the 4th of July. It starts simply
enough with seeing friends or an artistic opportunity. It continues to being a vacation like few others where masking is
replaced with community. Then deeper, the deep human itches of being understood and communing with a group of people you
can just exist wholly genuinely with.
